## Overview

Lolth is the goddess of spiders, shadows, deceit, ambition, and ruthless survival. She embodies a world where power is taken, where trust is a liability, and where strength is proven through dominance over others. To Lolth, existence is not meant to be fair or just. It is a web, and those who thrive are those who learn how to weave it.

She teaches that betrayal is not a failing, but a tool. Alliances are temporary, loyalty is conditional, and mercy is weakness unless it serves a greater purpose. Every creature exists within a hierarchy, whether they acknowledge it or not, and the only meaningful position is at the top.

Lolth’s influence is most strongly felt among the drow, though she is neither their creator nor tied to any divine lineage within Faysel. Instead, she is something they found or were found by, a force that resonated with their ambition and shaped their society into one of calculated cruelty and constant intrigue.

## Depictions in Art and Myth

Lolth is most often depicted as a dark, imposing figure with the lower body of a massive spider and the upper body of a strikingly beautiful but cold and severe humanoid woman. Her skin matches that of drow elves, her eyes glow red with quiet malice and awareness. She is frequently adorned in elaborate, web-like garments or armor that seem both regal and predatory.

In many depictions, she is surrounded by intricate webs that stretch beyond the frame of the image, suggesting that what is seen is only a fragment of her influence. These webs are often populated with struggling figures, bound prey, or symbols of power caught in her design.

Some myths describe her appearing fully as a spider, vast and unknowable, lurking just beyond perception. Others claim she walks among her followers in a more subtle form, indistinguishable from a mortal until the moment she reveals herself.

Her presence is rarely explosive or overwhelming. Instead, it is suffocating and inevitable, like realizing too late that one has already been caught.

## Goals

Lolth seeks the perpetuation of struggle. She does not desire a stable dominion where her followers rule without challenge. Instead, she cultivates environments of constant tension, where ambition drives conflict and conflict produces strength.

She encourages her followers to scheme, betray, and compete, not out of chaos for its own sake, but because she believes that only through relentless pressure can true power emerge. A society that becomes too stable is, in her eyes, already decaying.

Worship of Lolth is not meant to provide comfort or protection. It is a pact of opportunity. Those who rise in her name do so through their own cunning and cruelty, and those who fall are simply unworthy.

She does not promise salvation. She offers a chance to take it.

## Relationships

Lolth exists largely outside the structure of Faysel’s primary pantheon. She is not counted among the major gods, and her presence is often treated as an intrusion rather than a natural part of divine order.

Gods such as Cyrus and Calypso view Lolth as fundamentally destabilizing. Where they represent structure, continuity, and inevitability, Lolth thrives on disruption and the deliberate unraveling of stability. She does not oppose them directly, but her influence undermines the systems they maintain.

Asmodeus finds Lolth both useful and distasteful. Both value manipulation and hierarchy, but where Asmodeus seeks rigid control and long-term order, Lolth embraces volatility. She builds systems designed to fracture, trusting that only the strongest will emerge from the collapse. Their philosophies intersect but never align.

Lolth has little meaningful interaction with gods such as Castor or Solara, whose domains rely on guidance, growth, and protection. These concepts hold little value in her worldview, where guidance is manipulation and growth is earned through suffering.

Among darker or more pragmatic deities, Lolth is regarded with caution. Even those who embrace cruelty recognize that her methods erode not just enemies, but allies as well. To engage with Lolth is to accept that one is always at risk of becoming expendable.

She has no true allies, only temporary convergences of interest. Every relationship, divine or mortal, is another thread in her web.

## Myth and History

Lolth’s origin in Faysel is uncertain and deliberately obscured. Some myths claim she emerged from the Shadowfell, a consciousness that coalesced around the concepts of ambition and predation. Others suggest she was once a lesser spirit that grew into divinity through the devotion and fear of those who found power in her teachings.

Among the drow, her rise is more clearly defined. Early accounts describe a fragmented people, divided and struggling for survival in hostile environments. Lolth’s influence did not unite them through peace or shared purpose, but through competition. She reshaped their culture into one where hierarchy was enforced through strength, and advancement required cunning, betrayal, or both.

This transformation was not immediate. It occurred over generations, as her teachings took root and reinforced themselves. Each act of treachery justified the next, each rise to power validating her philosophy. Over time, what began as influence became identity.

Outside of drow society, Lolth’s presence is more subtle. She appears in whispers of political intrigue, in sudden betrayals that collapse alliances, and in individuals who rise rapidly through manipulation rather than merit. Her touch is rarely obvious, but its effects are unmistakable.

Unlike many gods, Lolth does not rely on widespread worship. A single devoted schemer can serve her purposes as effectively as an entire temple. Her power lies not in numbers, but in the complexity of the webs she weaves.

In the broader history of Faysel, Lolth is not remembered for grand acts or world-shaping events. She is remembered for fractures. Kingdoms that fell from within, alliances that dissolved at their peak, and leaders who turned on those closest to them at the precise moment it mattered most.
