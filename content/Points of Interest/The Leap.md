## Overview 

The Leap is one of Seabrook’s oldest and most sacred landmarks. Set atop an outcropping of cliffs in [[Old Seabrook]] nearly a hundred feet above the churning water below. It overlooks the [[Siren Sea]] and serves as both a mourning ground and a place of catharsis. Traditionally, when a sailor or fisher is lost at sea, their loved ones leap from the cliff into the waters below to symbolically reunite with them. The waters beneath are deep and free of rocks, making the jump survivable, though not without danger. Some choose to leap alone. Others do so as part of a ceremony, with the townsfolk watching from the benches above, bells from the [[Temple of Iskandar (Seabrook)]] tolling in the distance. Several stone benches stand near this precipitous edge, and a few stone markers sit in the tall grass nearby.
