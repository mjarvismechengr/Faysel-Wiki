## Overview

The Wanakona Compact is a maritime union of island realms spread across the shattered continent of [[Wanakona]]. Governed from the cliffside capital of [[Amytherian]], the Compact directly controls the largest and most populated islands of the archipelago, while hundreds of smaller islands remain only loosely connected to its authority. Many remote communities may go years without seeing a national official, yet still consider themselves Wanakonan through shared ancestry, tradition, and allegiance to the ancient Compact.

Wanakona is known throughout the world as the birthplace of dragons. Dragonborn form the clear majority of its population, while dragonelles are commonly raised as mounts, work animals, and aerial couriers. True dragons dwell throughout the archipelago, particularly among the remote islands and the floating peaks of the [[Mistspires]]. Most remain apart from mortal society, though several metallic dragons maintain relationships with the government, and one ancient dragon serves as the Compact’s sovereign and guardian.

The nation is bound together by necessity as much as identity. Wanakona’s waters are difficult to navigate, its weather is unpredictable, and planar rifts open throughout the islands with little warning. The people have therefore developed a culture centered on seamanship, oral history, mutual rescue, and practical knowledge passed between generations.

## Culture

Wanakona is a culture of sailors, navigators, herders, quarry workers, craftspeople, and island communities accustomed to surviving with limited outside aid. Its people prize reliability, practical skill, and the ability to remain calm when familiar rules cease to apply.

Navigation is among the most respected professions in the Compact. Wanakonan sailors learn not only currents, stars, and coastlines, but also the signs of magical disruption. Rifts can alter weather, distort distance, interfere with compasses, and make familiar landmarks unreliable. Many safe routes are therefore preserved through songs, recited histories, carved markers, and knowledge passed directly from navigator to apprentice.

Static maps are treated as useful tools, but rarely as unquestionable truth.

The islands share a common Wanakonan identity while maintaining distinct local cultures. Dialects, laws, rituals, clothing, food, and methods of leadership can vary dramatically from one island to the next. An island governed by a hereditary chief may lie only a short voyage from one ruled by a council of sea captains, clan elders, or elected navigators.

These differences have led to old rivalries and occasional wars, but they are also regarded as part of what it means to be Wanakonan. Most islanders view the Compact as a promise of mutual aid rather than permission for Amytherian to govern every aspect of their lives.

To outsiders, the Wanakona Compact is often regarded as a distant and weather-beaten backwater perched at the edge of the known world. This reputation is only partly deserved. The nation lacks the great armies, roads, and monumental cities of larger powers, but its hostile waters, planar instability, dragonelle squadrons, and extraordinary concentration of true dragons make the archipelago nearly impossible to conquer.

### Daily Life

Most Wanakonans live far more ordinary lives than the archipelago’s reputation suggests. Fishing, herding, forestry, quarrying, shipbuilding, seaweed cultivation, and inter-island trade support much of the population. Coastal settlements maintain docks, beacon towers, communal storehouses, and shelters intended for storms, dragon attacks, or planar emergencies.

Dragonelles are a familiar part of life throughout the larger islands. Wealthy families may keep them for travel, while farmers, couriers, guides, and patrols use them much as people elsewhere use horses. Skilled military and Riftguard riders remain prestigious, but simply riding a dragonelle is not unusual.

True dragons are treated very differently. They are intelligent, powerful individuals rather than beasts or subjects of the state. Many claim remote islands, mountain peaks, or sections of the Mistspires as their territory. Some are trusted allies, others are dangerous enemies, and many simply wish to be left alone. Wanakonans learn from childhood that knowing whose territory one is entering can be as important as knowing the weather.

### Dragonborn and Outsiders

Dragonborn trace their origin to the same ancient upheaval that created the dragons. According to accepted history, they were created by [[Manai]] as soldiers during the destruction that ended the [[Age of Wonder]]. Wanakona has remained their ancestral homeland ever since.

Other peoples live throughout the Compact, particularly in Amytherian, where foreign merchants, scholars, sailors, and representatives of the [[Candelabra]] are common. They are generally accepted under Wanakonan law, though integration varies greatly by island.

Amytherian is comparatively cosmopolitan. In smaller or more isolated communities, outsiders may struggle to purchase land, participate in local rites, hold traditional office, or be trusted with navigational knowledge. Such restrictions are rarely uniform national policy, but are instead rooted in local custom and longstanding suspicion of foreign interference.

### Magic and the Rifts

Magic is familiar to most Wanakonans even when they cannot practice it themselves. Communities near unstable rifts maintain warning systems, evacuation plans, and local traditions for recognizing planar danger. Children may be taught which colors of mist to avoid, how to react to sudden changes in gravity, or why voices heard from an empty stretch of sea must never be answered.

Most rifts connect to the Elemental Planes, though breaches into the Feywild, Shadowfell, and stranger realms are also known. Some remain stable for generations, while others open without warning and disappear within hours.

Planar-touched individuals occupy an uncertain place within Wanakonan society. Those who gain useful abilities may be respected as navigators, wardens, or sorcerers, but visible mutations and unpredictable magic can also inspire fear. Draconic sorcery is rare and prestigious, often regarded as evidence of an unusually strong connection to Wanakona’s ancient origins.

## Government

The Wanakona Compact is governed through a collection of ancient treaties, oral oaths, traditional rulings, and later laws accumulated across centuries. A modern document known simply as the National Compact summarizes the structure of the nation, but it is not the sole source of legal authority. Older agreements remain binding, and disputes may depend upon promises preserved only through oral histories or judgments issued by long-dead sovereigns.

The government consists of three primary authorities: the national council, the High Steward, and the dragon sovereign.

### The National Council

The national council gathers the rulers and representatives of Wanakona’s recognized islands, settlements, and institutions.

The largest island polities hold permanent seats through their traditional rulers or appointed representatives. The form of those governments varies widely. One island may be led by a hereditary chief, another by a council of captains, another by clan elders, and another by an elected civic servant.

Amytherian and several other major settlements also send civic representatives. Additional seats may be held by outer-island delegates, military institutions, or offices granted representation through ancient service to the Compact.

The council is deliberately uneven. Some small islands possess rights secured centuries ago, while larger communities that joined later may hold comparatively little influence. Attempts to reform the system regularly meet resistance from rulers who fear that Amytherian will use population and wealth to strip the islands of their autonomy.

Ordinary laws, budgets, and appointments are decided by the wider council. Changes affecting the rights and obligations of the major islands require the approval of their traditional seats.

### The High Steward

The High Steward is appointed by the council to oversee the daily administration of the Compact. The office directs foreign policy, national finances, customs enforcement, chameleite regulation, and cooperation between the navy and the Riftguard.

The High Steward does not possess the authority to abolish local laws or directly rule the member islands. Much of the office’s practical power comes instead from control over national funding, military deployment, trade licenses, and the bureaucracy centered in Amytherian.

The High Steward must constantly balance the demands of the capital against the rights of distant island rulers. Supporters of stronger national authority argue that unified law is necessary to control smuggling, defend the archipelago, and respond to planar threats. Opponents fear that each new national institution weakens the independence promised by the Compact.

The current High Steward is [[Zeskonuz]].

### The Dragon Sovereign

The symbolic head of the Compact is a true dragon chosen to serve for life as guardian of the national union.

When the position becomes vacant, Wanakona’s recognized dragons nominate candidates from among their kind. The national council then selects the new sovereign. Any true dragon is legally eligible, though metallic dragons have overwhelmingly held the office due to their greater willingness to cooperate with mortal communities.

The sovereign does not normally govern daily affairs. Their purpose is to arbitrate disputes between island rulers, preserve the founding agreements, and intervene when the Compact faces a common existential threat. During foreign invasion, civil war, catastrophic planar breach, or similar crisis, the sovereign may assume extraordinary authority over the nation’s defense.

This power is intentionally broad and poorly defined. Political disputes frequently arise over what qualifies as a threat to the entire Compact and when the sovereign is justified in acting.

The current sovereign, [[Yvrym]], is an ancient bronze dragon whose interests reflect both the sea and the preservation of knowledge. Their hoard includes salvaged ships, lost maps, experimental devices, recovered histories, and objects rescued from destroyed settlements. Though widely respected, their support for scholarship, exploration, and cooperation between the islands is sometimes viewed by traditional rulers as quiet encouragement of Amytherian’s growing influence.

The sovereign resides on a rocky island just offshore from Amytherian. A wind-beaten audience hall stands above the water, while the dragon’s true lair extends through sea caves beneath it. The island belongs to the office of the sovereign and lies outside the jurisdiction of both the capital and the ruler of the main island.

## Kawanui, The Main Island

The largest island of Wanakona, [[Kawanui]], serves as both the cultural heart of the archipelago and the center of the national government. Its five best-known settlements are [[Amytherian]], [[Inver]], [[Blackstone]], [[Hebhuron]], and [[Dragonwatch]].

The island is governed according to an old custodial tradition founded on the belief that land is not truly ruled, but entrusted to those responsible for preserving it. Regional custodians represent the island’s major settlements and responsibilities, selecting from among themselves a First Keeper to speak for the island on the national council.

The First Keeper is not a monarch. The office mediates between settlements, protects traditional land rights, maintains relationships with dragons, and ensures that the island’s ancient obligations are upheld.

Amytherian governs its own urban affairs and sends separate civic representatives to the national council. Relations between the city and the First Keeper are generally cooperative, though conflicts arise over trade, taxation, expansion, chameleite revenue, and the reach of national law.

Each region of the island maintains a distinct identity:

- [[Amytherian]] is the cliffside capital, center of government, foreign trade, and planar scholarship.
- [[Hebhuron]] is an old coastal settlement renowned for fishing, rescue crews, ship repair, and traditional navigation.
- [[Dragonwatch]] guards the northern approaches toward [[Al'Dira]] and maintains forts, beacons, and aerial patrols.
- [[Inver]] stands near the southeastern forests and is associated with timber, farming, hunting, dragonelle breeding, and old inland communities.
- [[Blackstone]] lies within the [[Black Marble Mountains]] and serves as the center of chameleite extraction and refinement.

## Chameleite

[[Chameleite]] is the Compact’s principal export and one of the most strategically valuable arcane materials in the world. It is quarried from pulverized black marble found in the [[Black Marble Mountains]], then refined into a dark violet, glass-like substance capable of amplifying enchantments.

The mountains are held in trust rather than owned outright by any single ruler or institution. Blackstone’s communities possess ancestral extraction rights, while the national government controls refining licenses, export quotas, and foreign sales. The First Keeper adjudicates traditional land disputes, and the Riftguard may suspend operations when mining threatens to disturb planar instability.

This overlapping authority is inefficient but intentional. Wanakonans are deeply wary of allowing control over chameleite to fall entirely into the hands of Amytherian, private guilds, foreign merchants, or any individual dragon.

Despite tight controls, smuggling is widespread. Raw stone, refined chameleite, and alchemical waste are transported through hidden coves and remote islands. Some of this material is used to create [[Morphesium|Morphe]], a highly addictive arcane drug that can dramatically heighten spellcasting while causing severe physical and magical harm.

Chameleite has made the Compact influential, but not uniformly wealthy. Much of the revenue is consumed by imports, national defense, Riftguard operations, maritime infrastructure, and the repeated cost of repairing settlements damaged by storms, dragons, and planar events.

## The Riftguard

The Riftguard is the national institution responsible for monitoring, containing, and responding to Wanakona’s planar breaches.

Its members include soldiers, mages, navigators, engineers, healers, researchers, and dragonelle riders. They maintain watch stations throughout the major islands, investigate planar disturbances, evacuate endangered settlements, fight extraplanar creatures, and attempt to close or stabilize dangerous rifts.

Not every rift is destroyed. Some provide access to useful resources, shorten dangerous journeys, or serve as sites of controlled research. The Riftguard therefore also regulates legal rift travel and determines whether a breach should be sealed, studied, exploited, or simply watched.

Its most prestigious aerial units operate from dragonelle-back, allowing them to reach remote islands and unstable terrain quickly. These riders are trained to navigate magical storms, shifting gravity, and the distorted distances surrounding active breaches.

The Riftguard works closely with the [[Umbral Observatory]] in Amytherian, home of the [[Candelabra]]’s Archmage of Exploration, [[Collete Moreau]]. Relations are generally cooperative, though disagreements arise when foreign scholars seek access to rifts the Riftguard considers too dangerous or politically sensitive.

## Military

The Compact maintains a national navy, local island militias, dragonelle squadrons, and the specialized forces of the Riftguard.

The navy protects major shipping routes, hunts pirates and smugglers, escorts chameleite shipments, and responds to threats that cross island boundaries. Individual islands maintain their own forces according to local tradition, ranging from professional guards and hereditary warrior companies to temporary fleets raised by councils of captains.

Dragonelle riders serve as scouts, couriers, coastal patrols, and aerial cavalry. While dragonelles themselves are common, military riders receive extensive training and are considered among the Compact’s most capable professional soldiers.

True dragons are not formally counted among the nation’s military forces. Recognized dragons may choose to defend Wanakona during an invasion, but they do so as independent allies rather than obedient weapons. Foreign powers nevertheless treat the archipelago’s dragon population as a major strategic threat. Any invasion risks provoking several ancient dragons, each powerful enough to devastate fleets or cities independently.

This uncertainty is among the Compact’s greatest defenses.

## History

### The Shattering of Wanakona

Wanakona was once connected to southern [[Al'Dira]], though little reliable history survives from that era. According to accepted tradition, the gods tore vast portions of land from both regions to create [[Manai]], leaving behind the [[Dragon Scar Sea]] and the broken archipelago that exists today.

The catastrophe fractured the land and weakened the boundaries between planes. Mountains rose into the sky, islands sank beneath the sea, and rifts opened throughout the surviving landscape. Dragons and dragonborn emerged during the same era, establishing Wanakona as their ancestral birthplace.

The [[Mistspires]] remain the most dramatic scar of this event: a vast region of floating mountains, drifting plateaus, and unstable gravity that dominates the southern reaches of the archipelago.

### The First Island Oaths

For much of its early history, Wanakona consisted of independent island peoples connected by trade, kinship, migration, and war. No ruler possessed the ships or authority necessary to govern the archipelago as a whole.

The beginnings of the Compact emerged through agreements between sailors and island rulers. Communities promised to rescue one another’s ships, maintain shared beacons, preserve navigational knowledge, and offer shelter during storms or planar events.

These customs gradually developed into binding oaths. Mutual-defense agreements followed as islands united against raiders, hostile dragons, extraplanar incursions, and foreign fleets.

The early Compact was not a centralized government. It was a promise that no member island would be left entirely alone when faced with a threat beyond its strength.

### The Union of the Isles

Over time, the largest islands began meeting through a common council. The office of the dragon sovereign arose to mediate disputes that mortal rulers could not resolve and to lead the islands during threats to the entire archipelago.

The council remained intentionally limited. Island leaders surrendered only those powers necessary for mutual defense, navigation, and foreign diplomacy. Attempts to expand national authority repeatedly led to rebellion, withdrawal, or threats of civil war.

The result was a nation held together not by conquest or uniform law, but by overlapping obligations that no single ruler fully controlled.

### The Chameleite Accords

The discovery of refined chameleite transformed Wanakona’s place in the world. Foreign merchants arrived in growing numbers, rival islands competed for control of the trade, and smugglers flourished among the countless coves and minor islands.

A series of conflicts over mining rights and export revenue nearly fractured the Compact. In response, the council negotiated the Chameleite Accords, placing foreign sales and refining licenses under national authority while preserving Blackstone’s ancestral rights and the main island’s custodial claims.

The accords greatly expanded the power of Amytherian and the national bureaucracy. They also established many of the political disputes that continue today.

### The Compact Today

The Wanakona Compact remains united, prosperous enough to endure, and difficult for any foreign power to threaten directly. Yet the balance between national authority and island autonomy grows increasingly strained.

Amytherian seeks stronger institutions to control smuggling, protect trade, and coordinate responses to planar danger. Many island rulers argue that each expansion of national power violates the spirit of the original oaths.

For now, the Compact holds. Its ships still answer distant distress calls, its beacons still burn through the mist, and its people remain bound by the belief that survival in Wanakona has always depended upon knowing when to stand alone and when to stand together.
