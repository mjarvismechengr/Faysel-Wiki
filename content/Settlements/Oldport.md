## Overview

Set between the shifting waters of the [[Siren Sea]] and the tangled sprawl of the [[West Marsh]], Oldport presents itself as one of the most orderly cities in the southern [[Kingdom of Thrane]]. Its docks are regulated, its tariffs documented, and its officials punctual and composed. Records are kept. Licenses are stamped. Inspections are thorough. On the surface, everything in Oldport is done by the book. That book, however, has many margins.

Oldport stands along the [[Siren Coast]] on the southern trade routes of [[Isomar]], guarding marsh access and sea lanes alike. Its strong walls and disciplined watch give the impression of stability, and compared to the wilder reaches of the marsh, it is stable. Ships are catalogued upon arrival. Cargo is weighed and logged. Disputes are settled in proper courts. The appearance of law is not false. It is simply selective.

Beneath this thin veneer, Oldport is a hub of smuggling, quiet theft, and backroom leverage. Contraband moves through legitimate warehouses. Ships declared lost at sea reappear under new ownership. Dock crews rotate in patterns that only make sense if someone is being paid to look the other way. The city functions because those who profit from order also profit from its careful violation.

Merchant houses such as [[Morbrand Mercantile]] maintain visible offices and heavily guarded ships in the harbor, publicly champions of secure trade. At the same time, lesser captains and discreet brokers use the same routes to move goods that never appear on any ledger. Whispers of a thieves’ guild known as the [[Silver Goblet]] circulate among sailors and warehouse workers, though nothing is ever proven and no one important is ever caught.

Towering above it all is the [[Widow's Light]], the ancient lighthouse whose golden beam cuts through fog and rumor alike. It is the city’s most visible symbol of guidance and safety, a reminder that Oldport is meant to be a place of refuge and commerce. Whether the light guards only ships, or something deeper beneath the harbor, depends on who is telling the story.
