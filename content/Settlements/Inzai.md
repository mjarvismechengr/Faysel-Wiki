## Overview

[[Inzai]] is the capital of [[Yoseju]] and the seat of the [[Shimazu Shogunate]]. It is one of the largest cities in the world, a sprawling metropolis of canals, broad processional roads, disciplined districts, and carefully ordered neighborhoods. Built along a coastal plain where river and the [[Siren Sea]] meet, Inzai stretches outward in deliberate rings, each layer reflecting its place within the hierarchy of the state.

At the center rises the shogunal compound, a fortified palace complex surrounded by moats, stone walls, and controlled gates. From there, the city radiates outward into administrative quarters, samurai estates, temple districts, merchant wards, artisan streets, and dense commoner neighborhoods. Bridges cross slow canals that divide wards by design. Movement through the city is structured. Guards are visible. Gates close at appointed hours. Order is not implied in Inzai. It is engineered.

The population of Inzai reflects the stratified society of Yoseju. Samurai households reside in planned compounds near their assigned posts. Artisans and scholars cluster around guild districts. Merchants occupy regulated marketplaces whose design limits fire, unrest, and unsanctioned growth. Peasant migrants and laborers fill tightly packed outer wards, supplying the labor that sustains the capital’s scale. Though highly structured, the city is vibrant. Markets are loud with negotiation, theaters and teahouses host poetry and performance, and seasonal festivals transform entire districts into choreographed spectacles of lantern light and ceremony.

Politically, Inzai is the physical manifestation of Kagetsu no Michi, the Way of Blossoms and Steel. Governance here is ritualized. Petitions pass through layered bureaucracy. Ceremonial audiences reinforce hierarchy. Punishments are public and formal. Advancement requires not only competence but composure. Clan rivalry plays out in subtle competition for prestige, marriages, and favor at court. Inzai is where ambition is sharpened into discipline.

Religiously, devotion to [[Rodikar]] in Yoseju’s refined interpretation permeates the capital. Warrior monasteries stand beside calligraphy schools. Training grounds sit within earshot of meditation halls. Strategy is studied as scripture. Even cuisine reflects philosophy. Court kitchens are laboratories of precision, where presentation, restraint, and seasonal balance communicate political intent as clearly as any decree.
