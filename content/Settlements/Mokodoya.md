## Overview

[[Mokodoya]] is the northernmost settlement of [[Yoseju]], perched along the wind cut coast of [[Tatakai Prefecture]] under the authority of [[Clan Sakai]]. It is a fishing village, a watchpost, and the closest inhabited land to the contested island of [[Mt. Mertari]]. 

Though often described as a military outpost, Mokodoya remains a working town. Boats leave at dawn. Nets are mended along the shore. Children run between storehouses built low against the wind. Yet soldiers are everywhere. Sakai marines drill on the beaches, signal fires are maintained along the hills, and watchtowers overlook both forest and sea. Every able body knows where to go when the horns sound.

Its defenses are strong for a settlement of its size. Earthwork walls, reinforced timber gates, and elevated platforms for archers ring the core of the village. Coastal batteries and hidden rock emplacements guard likely landing sites. The design favors containment and delay rather than grandeur. Mokodoya is not meant to win a war. It is meant to buy time.

Life here is shaped by proximity to holy war. Raids from the north are not constant, but they are expected. The people have adapted to this rhythm. Homes are built to be repaired quickly. Supplies are stored in distributed caches. Festivals are celebrated with quiet defiance rather than spectacle. To live in Mokodoya is to accept that history may arrive by longship without warning.

Strategically, the town is the tip of the spear for the [[Shimazu Shogunate]]. It absorbs the first shock of escalation and provides the first report of movement across the sea. If Mokodoya stands, Yoseju has warning. If it falls, the war has already moved south.
